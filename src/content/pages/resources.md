### What Makes Artiore Different from a Standard AI Interface

Most AI tools return answers. Artiore returns structured reasoning — Every response has five parts: Evidence (what is known), Assumptions (what is not yet verified), Trade-offs (the real costs of each path), Practical next steps (options you can act on), and Limits (what this analysis cannot decide). A confidence level — High, Medium, or Low — is shown with the response. Final judgment stays with you.

Key Insight

"The difference is not capability — it is architecture. An answer without structure is a conclusion without a path. You cannot audit it, challenge it, or build on it."

### The Six Gates: How Every Artiore Response Is Evaluated

Before any response is issued, it passes through six sequential gates: Truth, Measure, Integrity, Safety, Directive, and Proof. A gate cannot be skipped. A gate cannot be partially passed.

## Glossary of Terms

Core terminology used across the Artiore system. Precise definitions — not marketing language.

Distortion

Any gap between the question as posed and the question that should be answered. Classified as Minor, Significant, or Critical.

Artifact

A usable output — a document, decision, framework, or draft — that remains after an exchange. The measure of a completed cycle.

Gate

A sequential evaluation checkpoint that every response must pass before being issued. Six gates. No skipping. No partial passage.

Confidence Level

A calibrated signal of certainty. Not a disclaimer — a structural indicator of how much additional information is required before acting.

Velocity Drift

The failure mode of responding before the gates have been run. Produces fast output that fails on accuracy, integrity, or safety.

Frame Capture

Accepting the questioner's framing without testing it. Produces answers to the wrong question with high confidence.

Eternal Loop

The four-pillar doctrine: Reason → Courage → Service → Justice → ∞. A continuum with no terminal state.

Distribution Engine

The system that calibrates response depth to question stakes. Simple questions receive simple answers. High-stakes questions receive complete answers.
